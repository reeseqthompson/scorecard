It all started one weekend in December 2024 when founder Quay Thompson finished cleaning his basement and noticed not only that his basement had carpet, but that there was room to swing a wedge. A few practice balls, a couple of old Tupperware bins, and one free weekend later, the basement was transformed into a nine-hole indoor golf course.

What began as a winter experiment quickly became a year-round pastime. It was fun, it improved his short game, and it proved that you do not need warm weather or daylight to improve your swing. When his son Reese (a recreational golfer and professional computer science engineer) came home for the holidays, the idea took shape. BasementGolfLeague.com was created as a place to design courses, record scores, and connect with other players and course managers who believe that golf is not just a game, it is a great way to inspire a recreational golfer to clean the basement.

Basement Golf League celebrates the creativity, precision, and mild absurdity of practicing golf indoors. With a little imagination, a pitching wedge, and the right size Tupperware, golf season never really ends.

Rules & Guidelines

Basement Golf is played indoors, usually in the part of the house your spouse has given up on. Each player uses a pitching wedge and four indoor practice balls. Chip your ball from the tee into the target — simple, elegant, almost golf.

1. The Setting

Basement Golf is played indoors, usually in the part of the house your spouse has given up on. The course typically features one tee spot (usually a patch of carpet or a mat) and one hole (a Tupperware bin, laundry basket, or other suitable household target) for each of the 9 holes.

2. The Equipment

Each player uses a pitching wedge and a set of four indoor practice balls, preferably the kind that won’t shatter picture frames or friendships. Real golf balls are prohibited, unless you’re trying to move out anyway.

3. The Objective

Chip your ball from the tee into the target. Simple. Elegant. Almost golf.

4. Scoring

  • Put your first ball on the tee.
  • Sink it on your first shot, that’s a 1. If you miss, drop your second ball on the tee and take another shot.
  • If you make your second shot, that’s a 2.
  • Third shot, 3.
  • Fourth shot, 4.
  • Miss all four, congratulations, you’ve just carded a 5.

5. The Mulligan Rule

There are no mulligans. This is Basement Golf, not the Masters. Or, think of the entire game as mulligans; but each one counts as a stroke.

6. The Course

A “course” typically consists of nine holes, creatively laid out around furniture, pets, and unfinished projects. Every basement is different, which is part of the fun. Obstacles are encouraged, especially if they increase the chance of chaos.

7. The Players

Any number of players may join, but if you can’t all swing a wedge without hitting each other, you’ve gone too far.

8. Etiquette

  • Calling “fore” is optional but always appreciated.
  • Players must wait until all balls have come to rest before retrieving them from behind the Christmas decorations.
  • Arguing over whether the ball “was going in if it hadn’t hit the wall” is considered part of the game. However, any ball that ends up in the correct target receptacle is considered a successful shot.

9. The Spirit of the Game

Basement Golf is about skill, laughter, and mild property damage. Keep it friendly, keep it fun, and remember, if you’re sweating, you’re probably doing it wrong.
